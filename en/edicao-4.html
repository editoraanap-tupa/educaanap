What is my house made of?

I build my house with mud, from start to finish. Your house takes many more materials! Shall we find out what they are and where each one comes from?

Build the house

The house is still only on paper. For each part that blinks, choose the right material and watch the house being built, piece by piece, starting with the foundation.

All building materials came from nature: from clay, sand, rocks, trees and ores. That's why every piece that goes to waste is a piece of nature wasted.

Where did this material come from?

Tap a material, on the left, and then the place it comes from, on the right.

What's left over from a building site?

Building, renovating and demolishing always leave leftovers: that's rubble. Careful: real rubble cuts, pierces and may contain dangerous products. Children watch from a distance and don't touch!

The building site

Tap the 6 spots and find out how each kind of waste can be reused and recycled.

Mixed waste becomes a problem. Sorted, it becomes material again! Brazilian rules divide rubble into classes: what becomes crushed stone, what is recycled in another factory and what is dangerous.

Each kind of waste in its bay

Read the waste item and choose the right bay.

Waste classes according to Conama Resolution no. 307/2002 (Resolução Conama nº 307/2002) and its amendments (nos. 348/2004, 431/2011, 448/2012 and 469/2015). There is also class C, for waste that cannot yet be feasibly recycled.

The 5 Rs of building

Careful builders follow five words that start with R: reduce, reuse, recycle, rethink and refuse. The best leftover is the one that never exists!

  • ReduceMeasure before buying and cutting.
  • ReuseThe old door goes to the new house.
  • RecycleOld concrete becomes new gravel.
  • RethinkCan we renovate instead of demolishing?
  • RefuseDon't bring to the site what pollutes.

From rubble to new material

At the recycling plant, rubble is sorted, broken into small pieces by a machine called a crusher and sifted. What was building waste becomes recycled sand and gravel, ready for another building.

Did you know?

Researchers are studying how to mix plant scraps and other materials into bricks and mortars. Things that would go in the trash can help build!

  • Rice huskThe husk left over from rice is tested in bricks and mortars.
  • Coconut fiberThe threads of the coconut husk are studied to reinforce materials.
  • Sugarcane bagasseWhat's left of crushed sugarcane is also part of the research.
  • EggshellGround very fine, it is tested in mortar mixes.
  • PET bottlePlastic from bottles appears in studies of new blocks.
  • Raw earth: adobe and rammed earthEarth walls, a very old technique, are being studied again.

Examples of materials studied in research cited in the postdoctoral report that gave rise to this issue (MARQUES, 2026).

Where does rubble go?

Rubble dumped on a vacant lot or by the river clogs storm drains, causes floods and attracts animals that spread disease. Rubble belongs at the drop-off point, in an authorized company's dumpster or back at the building site.

          About the project

          The Outreach Project Urban and Environmental Literacy (Alfabetismo e Letramento Urbano e Ambiental) is an initiative of the Graduate Program in Architecture and Urbanism at UNIVAG (PPGAU-UNIVAG), carried out in partnership with ANAP – Associação Amigos da Natureza da Alta Paulista and linked to the Research Group City, Society and Landscape (Cidade, Sociedade e Paisagem).

          The project aims to bring the university, schools and society closer together, spreading and popularizing scientific knowledge about cities, the environment, landscape and sustainability in language that children can understand.

          Basic urban and environmental literacy refers to a child's ability to know, recognize and understand the basic elements, concepts and processes related to the city and the environment. It is building the repertoire a child needs to be able to “read” the place where they live.

          Urban and environmental literacy in practice is the next step: it refers to the ability to use this knowledge to interpret, connect, question and take a stand on real situations in their area, and even to propose solutions and take part in actions that bring change.

          One of the project's products is ECO LEGAL Interactive, a digital educational publication whose issues turn knowledge and findings from undergraduate research, master's dissertations, faculty research and postdoctoral studies into interactive content for children.

          Each issue covers a specific theme, combining scientific knowledge, educational language, visuals, games and activities to help develop urban and environmental literacy.

          Issue summary

          Theme

          The issue deals with construction waste and sustainable building: what a house is made of, where materials come from, what is left over from a building site, how to sort, reduce, reuse and recycle rubble and how a house can save water and energy. It is an extension product derived from Maurício Dias Marques's postdoctoral research, carried out at PPGAU-UNIVAG.

          Objective

          To help children understand that building materials come from nature and that the waste from a building site, when sorted, can get a new life.

          Specific objectives

          • Recognize the materials of a house and where each one comes from.
          • Identify the waste from a building site and sort it correctly.
          • Apply the 5 Rs to building situations.
          • Recognize the right destination for rubble and the solutions that make a house more sustainable.

          Methodology

          The issue adopts game-based learning, in three moments that repeat in each section: the mascot introduces the theme in a short speech, with the key words highlighted; the child plays, tests hypotheses and makes decisions; and each answer gets feedback that explains why, so that mistakes also teach.

          The activities follow the progression of urban and environmental literacy: identifying the elements, understanding the relationships among them, deciding and participating. In this issue, the path follows the life of materials: from nature to the building site, from the site to waste and from waste to a new use.

          The language is simple, the drawings are stylized, and there are accessibility features: audio of the speech and instructions, and translation into Libras (Brazilian Sign Language) via the VLibras Suite in the Portuguese version. Progress is tracked with stars and ends with the mission of the week, to do at home, and the diploma. The issue is aligned with the BNCC skills (BRASIL, 2018) and the 2030 Agenda targets (NAÇÕES UNIDAS, 2015), listed in the panels below.

          Rationale for the activities

          Build the house and Where did this material come from?
          They start with what children know, the house, and take them to where materials come from in nature. It is the starting point for understanding why not to waste.
          The building site and Each kind of waste in its bay
          They show what is left over from a building site and practice sorting waste, which is what makes reuse and recycling possible.
          Which R is it?
          It organizes actions into five verbs (reduce, reuse, recycle, rethink and refuse) and asks children to classify concrete situations.
          The second life of materials and The right destination
          They link each waste item to what it can become and where it should go, and show the consequences of improper disposal.
          The house of the future and Waste detective
          These are synthesis and decision activities: building, on a limited budget, a house that saves water and energy, and recognizing mistakes on a building site.
          Quiz, Word search and Memory game
          They review and reinforce the issue's vocabulary and concepts.

          Skills from Brazil's National Common Core Curriculum (Base Nacional Comum Curricular, BNCC) (BRASIL, 2018) that this issue's activities help develop, from 1st to 5th grade. Tap a code to read the text of the skill.

          Materials and where they come from

          Build the house and Where did this material come from?

          • EF02CI01Science, 2nd grade: identify what materials everyday objects are made of and which materials they were made from in the past.
          • EF02CI02Science, 2nd grade: propose the use of different materials according to their properties, such as hardness and transparency.
          • EF03GE05Geography, 3rd grade: identify minerals and other products extracted from nature.
          • EF04GE08Geography, 4th grade: describe the process of production, circulation and consumption of different products.

          Waste, reuse and recycling

          The building site, Each kind of waste in its bay, The 5 Rs of building and The second life of materials

          • EF01CI01Science, 1st grade: compare the materials of everyday objects, discussing their origin, disposal and conscious use.
          • EF03GE08Geography, 3rd grade: relate the production of trash to excessive consumption and propose reduction, reuse and recycling.
          • EF05CI05Science, 5th grade: build proposals for conscious consumption, proper disposal, reuse and recycling.

          Rubble and the city

          • EF05GE11Geography, 5th grade: identify environmental problems around the school and home and propose solutions.
